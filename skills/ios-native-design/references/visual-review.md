# Visual review

Static source cannot determine whether a screen actually feels polished. Render first.

Review in this order:

1. **Purpose** — can you tell what the screen is for?
2. **Focus** — what attracts the eye first, and is it correct?
3. **Hierarchy** — are primary content, supporting content, metadata, and actions distinguishable?
4. **Platform familiarity** — do navigation and controls read as recognizable iOS patterns?
5. **Density** — is complexity coming from the task or unnecessary chrome?
6. **Alignment** — are there stable edges and optical alignment?
7. **Typography** — are roles consistent without headings competing?
8. **Color** — is accent color selective and muted content still legible?
9. **Materials** — does material establish a functional layer rather than decorate everything?
10. **States** — do loading, empty, error, selected, disabled, and success belong to one system?
11. **Motion** — does it clarify what changed?
12. **Accessibility** — review large text and system settings, not only the default screenshot.

## Severity

- **Blocker** — broken usability, serious accessibility failure, obscured content, or clearly wrong platform interaction.
- **Major** — hierarchy, consistency, density, component-choice, or readability issue that materially lowers quality.
- **Minor** — optical polish or small consistency issue.

## Design smells

Look for excessive rounded cards, borders, blur/glass, competing accent colors, giant low-value headings, tiny gray labels, almost-native custom controls, inconsistent margins/radii, mixed icon languages, unjustified floating buttons, hidden primary actions, and animation that competes with the task.

A polished Apple-native screen tends to feel understandable, calm, consistent, responsive, familiar where behavior matters, and expressive where product content matters.
